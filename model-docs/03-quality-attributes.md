# DRY Quality Attributes

DRY Quality Attributes are the evaluation criteria for the Data Platform DRY Model. They define the capability dimensions along which reuse can succeed or fail at each layer and reuse interface. Their role in the model is to underpin **Operational Maturity** assessment: the M0–M3 scale that measures whether the organization actually achieves reuse in practice.

Attributes such as Reusability, Abstraction, Composability, and Structure & Modularity are kept separate despite conceptual overlap because each fails independently in practice: an artifact can be highly reusable yet poorly abstracted (a leaky interface), or well-abstracted yet not composable (a monolithic surface), and collapsing them would hide the specific bottleneck.

| Id | Quality Attributes Group | Quality Attributes Subgroup | Quality Attribute | Expected Capability | Artifacts it applies to | Maturity Levels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engineering Enablement | Reusability Mechanisms | **Reusability** | Logic can be defined once and referenced across multiple contexts by design, independent of whether reuse is enforced or adopted | All | M0: No native reuse<br>M1: Pattern-based, informal reuse<br>M2: Reusable components with explicit references<br>M3: First-class reusable constructs |
| 2 | Engineering Enablement | Reusability Mechanisms | **Abstraction** | Implementation complexity (including how business meaning is operationalized) is encapsulated behind stable, well-defined interfaces that simplify consumption | All | M0: Inline complexity<br>M1: Partial abstractions<br>M2: Clear interfaces enforced<br>M3: Standardized abstraction layers |
| 3 | Engineering Enablement | Reusability Mechanisms | **Parametrization** | Behavior can be configured via parameters without requiring code changes. Parametrization is critical and structural for callable logic, intentionally constrained for datasets, and filter-based for semantic contracts. | All | Strength varies by interface type<br><br>M0: Hardcoded logic<br>M1: Manually supplied parameters<br>M2: Validated and constrained configurations<br>M3: Policy-driven configuration |
| 4 | Engineering Enablement | Reusability Mechanisms | **Composability** | Small, reusable units can be combined into higher-order comprehensive solutions, without duplicating logic or redefining meaning | All | M0: Monolithic logic<br>M1: Partial composition<br>M2: Modular composition<br>M3: Ecosystem of composable units |
| 5 | Engineering Enablement | Engineering Quality & Maintainability | **Structure & Modularity** | Logic is organized into clear and logically cohesive units | All | M0: Flat scripts<br>M1: Logical grouping<br>M2: Strong module boundaries<br>M3: Domain-aligned modular architecture |
| 6 | Engineering Enablement | Engineering Quality & Maintainability | **Testability** | Correctness can be validated through automated unit and integration tests. Includes both code tests and data/semantic validation, depending on artifact type. | All | M0: No tests<br>M1: Manual testing<br>M2: Automated tests exist and run in CI<br>M3: Automated tests protect correctness and, where applicable, semantic intent; failures block unsafe promotion and prevent unintended regressions |
| 7 | Engineering Enablement | Engineering Quality & Maintainability | **Portability** | The artifact is portable across platforms with minimal refactoring required to preserve the abstraction’s behavior (implementation is not vendor-locked). | All | M0: Platform-locked (full rewrites needed)<br>M1: Minor refactors needed<br>M2: Multi-platform ready<br>M3: Platform-agnostic by design |
| 8 | Engineering Enablement | Engineering Quality & Maintainability | **Execution Efficiency & Scalability** | Evaluates whether the reuse mechanism integrates cleanly with the execution engine and optimizer, without forcing inefficient execution patterns (e.g., deep view chains, row-by-row execution, repeated recomputation). This criterion evaluates structural execution characteristics, not query tuning quality or manual optimization effort. | All | M0: Structural execution inefficiencies<br>M1: Suboptimal execution<br>M2: Clean engine/optimizer integration<br>M3: Execution efficiency remains stable as reuse scales |
| 9 | Engineering Enablement | Reuse Enforceability Mechanisms | **Reuse Enforceability** | The artifact makes correct reuse the natural or lowest-friction path through named, constrained, discoverable, or executable interfaces, while making duplication harder, more visible, or easier to govern. Distinct from lifecycle management governing change propagation and dependency impact. | All | M0: No enforcement; duplication is the default<br>M1: Reuse encouraged through guidance and best practices<br>M2: Enforced interfaces exist; reuse remains optional<br>M3: Structural enforcement; reuse is the default path; platform or CI/CD controls use artifact interfaces and metadata to detect high-confidence duplication and route it to required review or exception approval |
| 10 | Governance & Operability | - | **Versioning and Lifecycle Management** | The artifact is version-controlled and deployed via CI/CD, with dependency tracking and impact analysis to prevent uncontrolled duplication and semantic forks during change propagation. | All | M0: Manual changes<br>M1: Version control only<br>M2: CI/CD enforced<br>M3: Consumer-aware artifact evolution prevents breaking changes and semantic forks |
| 11 | Governance & Operability | - | **Discoverability & Metadata Visibility** | The artifact is searchable and contextually visible via catalogs, lineage graphs, and semantic search. Relates to both “engineering” and “business” discoverability depending on the artifact. | All | M0: Tribal knowledge<br>M1: Basic documentation<br>M2: Catalog + lineage<br>M3: Contextual discovery at design and consumption time in IDEs and BI tools, with semantic awareness |
| 12 | Governance & Operability | - | **Service Exposure Capability** | The artifact can be exposed through a standardized consumption interface (e.g., SQL endpoint, semantic query layer, or API), enabling cross-tool and cross-domain reuse beyond direct code imports. | N/A: Reusable Logic Artifacts (functions are imported, not served) | M0: Not exposed<br>M1: Ad-hoc, tool-specific exposure<br>M2: Standardized service layer<br>M3: Productized, governed consumption interfaces |
| 13 | Organizational Semantic Alignment | - | **Semantic Alignment** | The artifact encodes and enforces consistent business meaning across teams and tools through explicit, governed semantic contracts. | N/A: Reusable Logic Artifacts | M0: Team-specific meaning<br>M1: Shared definitions exist but can be bypassed<br>M2: Semantic enforcement exists at query or consumption time, but adoption remains optional<br>M3: Semantic contracts are mandatory for Tier-1 (business-critical) use cases; bypass requires explicit exception |

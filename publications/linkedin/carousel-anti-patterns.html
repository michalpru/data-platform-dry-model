Reuse in data platforms

Reuse rarely breaks loudly.

No alert.

No failed run.

Just the same code, business logic, and metrics quietly reimplemented across the platform.

ANTI-PATTERN 01

The final_v2 table

A table that began as orders_clean became orders_clean_final after someone's hotfix, and eventually orders_clean_final_finance_2026Q1 — a finance-specific version for one reporting period.

Each variant encodes a slightly different business rule. None are deprecated. All are still queried in production.

The real problem

No version is authoritative, and nothing retires the old ones.

ANTI-PATTERN 02

The business rule
living in the dashboard

Net Revenue was created as a calculated field inside a Power BI report because it was faster than putting it in a shared model. It reached the executive dashboard. Three other teams then built their own versions in their own reports.

The real problem

When the business rule changes, there is no canonical place to update and no way to know how many different versions exist.

ANTI-PATTERN 03

The same ingestion logic
rebuilt by every team

One team built Salesforce ingestion: OAuth refresh, retries, pagination, deserialization, error handling. Another team rebuilt the same for Zendesk. Another for Stripe. The source-specific parts were genuinely different. The surrounding mechanics were not.

The real problem

No shared ingestion module. The generic parts are rebuilt for every source.

ANTI-PATTERN 04

The notebook that
became production

An analyst built an LTV calculation one weekend, connecting to both data warehouse and source datasets directly. Leadership cited the numbers in a board deck. Six months later, the notebook runs on a schedule, feeding the whole organization. In the meantime an upstream table was silently renamed without anyone noticing the breakage.

The real problem

No owner, no tests, and no way to catch upstream changes before they break it.

ANTI-PATTERN 05

The superior view
nobody wants to touch

It started as a simple filter on the orders table. Over three years it absorbed JOINs, CASE WHEN logic, and rules no one remembers writing. It is now a 700-line SQL statement that feeds 40 downstream queries, 5 dashboards, and 2 ML pipelines, and no one is certain what it actually computes.

The real problem

It became canonical by accident: untestable, unowned, and too risky to change.

Why reuse breaks at scale

Local reimplementation is simply easier, and sometimes the only available path.

These are not team failures.
They are systemic outcomes of how data platforms are organized, governed, and consumed.
